# Designing for the feed

Your game isn't launched — it's *scrolled into*. The player has already seen ten games this minute. These are the things that make one stick.

## The attract screen is your thumbnail

Short videos win in the first second; so do games. Your attract screen is what players see before they decide.

- **Move.** A demo playing itself, an idle animation — anything but a static title card.
- **Show the goal.** The player should understand what to do before tapping.
- **Say "tap".** One short prompt, near the middle of the screen.

## Respect the insets

`tc.init()` returns two inset sets. In `preview` the feed's overlay covers the top tabs, the action rail on the right (about 72 pt) and the caption at the bottom-left (about 148 pt). Keep your title and your "tap to play" clear of them. During play (`insets.play`) only the status bar is covered; the Dock sits *below* your viewport, never on top of it. Your viewport never resizes.

## Runs of 20–90 seconds

Most sessions on Thumbcade are a run or three. Ramp difficulty early, end runs decisively, and make *Play again* irresistible. Long games work too — set `session` honestly and save progress with `tc.storage`.

## Feel

- Easing, particles, screen shake and hit-stop make tiny games feel expensive.
- Use `tc.feedback()` for the moments that matter — a hit, a win — not every frame.
- Audio starts muted in the feed until the player unmutes. Your game must be fun silent.
